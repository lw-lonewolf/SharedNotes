In order to understand the user deeply you need qualitative research. 
* **QUANTITATIVE** research answers "how much" or "how many". It often misses nuances of human behavior
* **QUALITATIVE** research answers *what, how , why* in detail. It helps in understanding: 
	* User behaviors, attitudes and goals.
	* The context in which the product will be used. 
	* Problem people face currently.

### QUALITATIVE RESEARCH METHODS
#### Ethnography 
Comes from anthropology. The researcher *immerses themselves in a group's natural setting* over a long period to study their behaviors, language and culture through observation and interviews.
* e.g. researcher spends months in a software startup's office, observing how developers collaborate, their coding practices, and their team dynamics to understand their "culture of coding"
#### Grounded Theory
Comes from sociology, instead of starting with theory, the researcher *builds* the theory from *ground up* by analyzing data like interviews to find themes and patterns 

* e.g researcher interviews many developers about experience with new tools. By analyzing the interviews they might develop a theory that explains the stages of tool adoption, based on recurring themes like "learning curves" or "community support"

#### Narrative Research
Comes from humanities, the researcher studies individuals' lives by asking them to narrate stories about their experiences.
* Conducting in-depth interviews with women in software engineering, asking them to share stories of their career path, including challenges and successes to understand their personal and professional journeys.
