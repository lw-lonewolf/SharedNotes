A common challenge for design is balancing the needs for beginners and experts. According to book, this is the wrong way to think about it. 
* The user base is not just beginners and experts, most users are intermediates between these two skill levels. 
* User skill levels follow a *bell curve*, a few beginners on the left, vast majority in the middle and few experts on the right. 
* The mistake is when programmers design for experts (like themselves) and then the marketing team demands features for absolute beginners. This means the application can either accommodate absolute beginners or expert users while ignoring the vast majority of users. 

### Threefold Goal of Design
* Help beginners become intermediates *quickly and painlessly*
* *Avoid blocking* intermediates who want to become experts
* *Keep perpetual intermediates* happy and productive. 

### Needs of Users by Type
#### Beginners 
* Sensitive and Easily Discouraged
* Intelligent but busy and want a *rapid, targeted learning process*
* They need to learn *how to operate* the product, not *how it works internally*
#### Experts
* They want *shortcuts* to everything 
* They appreciate *new powerful features* and are not bothered by added complexity. 
* Their opinions are very influential on less experienced users, so their needs can not be ignored
#### Perpetual Intermediate 
* They want a "working set" of intermediate tools and demand that these are *front and easy to find*
* They don't need basic concepts explained repeatedly but they appreciate helpful, non-intrusive aids like tool tips. 
* They know how to use reference material like online help etc.
