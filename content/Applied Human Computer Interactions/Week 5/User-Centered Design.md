GQM ->  *GOAL-QUESTION-METRIC*

It is a structured approach used to define and measure objectives, often applied in software engineering and usability evaluation

**GOAL**: Define what you want to achieve (user satisfaction, bug count etc.)

**QUESTION**: (How quickly can users complete a task?)

**Metric**: (Task Completion Time, Number of Errors etc.)

## Key Idea

**Users and their needs must be central to project requirements and development.**  
Goal: Make sure **design solves real user problems** and fits naturally into their work.

## Intent & Meaning

- **Shared responsibility:** Everyone on the team contributes to solving usability and interface issues — not just designers.
    
- **Leverage domain knowledge:** Understand users’ environment, goals, and workflows so the product fits _them_, not the other way around.
    
- **Challenge:** Designing good solutions is hard — it requires asking the right questions and iterating.

## Core Questions 
- **Who are my users?** (Identify personas or roles)
- **What are they trying to accomplish?** (Understand goals)
- **How do they think about the task?** (Mental models)
- **What experiences do they find enjoyable/rewarding?**
- **How should my product behave?** (Expected behavior)
- **What form should it take?** (UI, interaction style)
- **How will users interact with it?** (Workflows, navigation)
- **How to organize functions effectively?**
- **How will first-time users be introduced?** (Onboarding)
- **How to make technology understandable & controllable?**
- **How will it handle user errors/problems?**
- **How will it support infrequent/inexperienced users?**

### TLDR 
User-centered design = Asking the right questions about users’ goals, experiences, and interactions — and making them part of every design decision.
