*Goal-Directed Design Process* is a user-centered design methodology focused on understanding a users goals, behaviors, and needs to create products that help them achieve those goals effectively.

* It *focuses* on **goals**, not just **tasks** - ***WHY?*** not just ***HOW?*** 
* *User Personas* - these are highly specific research-based representations of user archetypes made in order to guide design decisions 

>[!NOTE] What is an Archetype?
>An *archetype* is a research-backed confirmed type of users that belong in a certain domain or sample of the population.

* *Iterative in nature* -> involves continuous refinement based on feedback from the users.
 
>[!error] Difference between Grounded Design and Goal-Directed Design Process
> > * *Grounded Design* focuses on existing user behavior, workflow and context and models are built around how users are already doing tasks. Feedback takes place once, at the end.
> > * *Goal-Directed Design* is based upon what the user *should* be able to accomplish, not just what they can currently do. Feedback is a continuous process.

## Problematic Design Processes 
Generally, *research* and *design* are isolated. *Research* is performed by analysts and the marketing department while *design* process is performed by graphics/GUI engineers. Most recently, *research* has started to include **qualitative and ethnographic** attributes but this is not enough. 

Without including designers in the research process, the connection between research and design will remain flawed.

>[!NOTE] What is Empathy?
>**Empathy** means putting one's self into someones else's shoes in order to better understand a problem or in our case design problem from the other persons perspective. i.e. understanding their emotions, feelings and thoughts of the users.

### Phases of the GDD Process
There are *six* phases in total: 
* *Research* users and domain
* *Modelling* of users and user context
* *Requirements* definition of user, business, and technical needs 
* *Framework* definition of design structure and flow 
* *Refinement* of behaviors, form and content 
* *Support* development needs 

>[!important] Remember the gap we talked about?
>There are three activities/processes that cover this gap in GDD: 
>* process of modeling that synthesizes research results into design tools.
>* process of procuring requirements from these models.
>* process of translating the knowledge into a design framework that reflect user needs and goals.

#### 1: RESEARCH PHASE
	Gather qualitative data through interviews, obeservations, and contextual inquiry.
	
During this phase you need to understand the *behavior* pattern of potential or actual users of the product. Research phase is directly responsible of the creation of *user personas* in the **MODELLING** phase. 
Activities in this phase include: *stakeholder interviews, literature reviews, product audits* etc.

#### 2: MODELLING PHASE
	Build personas and workflow models based on research findings.
-> DOMAIN MODELS
	Information Workflows and workflow diagrams etc. 
-> Personas
	Detailed composite user archetypes representing behaviors, attitudes, goals, motivations observed and identified during research.

#### 3: REQUIREMENTS DEFINITION PHASE
	Identify user goals and derive the functional requirements
* Focus on meeting the goals and needs of the specific user personas. 
* Focus should **not** be on **code-able user task** instead it should be on understanding the importance of each task and why they are important for the user.
* There should be a balance between user requirements, business requirements and technical requirements.
#### 4: FRAMEWORK PHASE 
	Create interaction models, information architectures, and conceptual
	design. 
* Using *interaction design principles*, *interaction design patterns*

#### 5: REFINEMENT PHASE
	Develop detailed design, including visual design and micro-interactions.
* Use *walkthroughs, validation scenarios, Story-boarding paths*

#### 6: DEVELOPMENT SUPPORT PHASE
	*Work with the developers in order to ensure the final product matches the
	design intent.

### GOALS NOT FEATURES
- **Developers build function by function**, but users care about achieving their goals — not the features themselves.

- **Feature lists describe capability**, but do not guarantee a good user experience.  
- **Measuring success means asking:**
  > Are users effective, satisfied, and happy while using the product?    
- **Designer’s job:**
  > Orchestrate technology so it helps users accomplish their goals in a natural, efficient way.
- **Problem with feature-driven design:**
   > Products often become a random collection of cool features driven by marketing or technical constraints — not by what users actually need.
- **Key takeaway:**
  > Design for **human goals and experiences**, not just technical functionality.
