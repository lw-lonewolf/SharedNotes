The goal is to construct a set of personas that represent the diversity of observed motivations, behaviors, attitudes, constraints, mental models, work flows, environments, and frustrations.

Personas must be based on **real-world observation**. The methodology is sometimes referred to as the **Stanislavsky Method of interaction design**.

**The 8 Steps:**

1. **Group interview subjects by role:** For enterprise applications, roles usually map to job roles; for consumer products, grouping is based on attitudes, approaches to activities, or lifestyle.
2. **Identify behavioral variables:** Derived by observing user characteristics, including: **Activities** (what the user does, frequency, volume), **Attitudes** (how the user thinks about the product domain/technology), **Aptitudes** (associated with education/training), **Motivations** (why the user is engaged), and **Skills** (user abilities related to the domain/technology).
3. **Map interview subjects to behavioral variables:** Subjects are mapped across each behavioral axis. The relative position of a subject is less important than their position relative to others.
4. **Identify significant behavior patterns:** Clusters of subjects across multiple axes indicate significant behavior patterns. A cluster across 6 to 8 different variables will likely form the basis for a persona.
5. **Synthesize characteristics and define goals:** Goals and attributes are derived from observed behaviors.
    - Details should be representative of a "day in the life".
    - Include: Behaviors (activities/motivations), user environment, frustrations/pain points, relevant demographics, skills/experience, attitudes/emotions, and alternate or competing ways of doing the same thing.
    - The authors strongly recommend giving the persona a name, age, geographic location, relative income, and job title.
    - Goals must relate directly to the product being designed.
    - Limit the fictional biography (it should not be a character sketch).
6. **Check for completeness and redundancy:** Ensure the persona "set" is complete (Did you miss anything?).
7. **Designate persona types (Prioritize):** Personas are prioritized to focus the design. The design should ideally find a single persona whose needs/goals can be completely and happily satisfied by a single interface without disenfranchising any other personas.
8. **Expand the description of attributes and behaviors (The Persona Narrative):**
    - The narrative should contain the majority of the user research.
    - It should be no longer than one or two pages of prose.
    - It should introduce the persona’s job and lifestyle and express what they are looking for in the product.
    - **Crucially:** No design thinking should be included at this point.
