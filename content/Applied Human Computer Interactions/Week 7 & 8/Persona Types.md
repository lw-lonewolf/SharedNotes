There are typically six types of personas, usually designated in the following rough order:

1. **Primary:** The main target of the interface design. All other personas need to be satisfied, but design targets this one first. If a consumer product has multiple primary personas, the scope may be too broad.
2. **Secondary:** Should be mostly satisfied with the primary persona’s interface. The design is adjusted to accommodate the secondary after designing for the primary. Multiple secondary personas may also indicate scope issues.
3. **Supplemental:** Not primary or secondary, but can be satisfied with the existing interfaces.
4. **Customer:** Addresses the needs of customers, not end users. In enterprise environments, customer personas may be primary personas for their own administrative interface.
5. **Served:** Not users of the product, but are directly affected by the use of the product (e.g., a patient treated by a radiation therapy machine). They help track second-order social and physical ramifications of products.
6. **Negative:** Personas that the product is **not** being developed to serve. Examples include criminals, less-harmful pranksters/trolls, or tech-savvy early-adopters for consumer products.
