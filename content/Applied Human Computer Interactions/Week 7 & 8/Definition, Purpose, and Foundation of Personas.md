**A. What Personas Are**

- Personas are **models of users**.
- They are developed to represent complex phenomena with a useful abstraction.
- Personas are represented as **individual people** but actually represent **groups of users**.
- They must be **typical and believable**, but **not stereotypical**.
- Personas explore ranges of behavior and must have **motivations**.
- They can also represent nonusers.

**B. Why Personas are Used (Design Tool Functions)**

1. **Determine:** Identify what the product should do and how it should behave.
2. **Communicate:** Provide stakeholders, developers, and designers with a common language for discussing design decisions, maintaining focus on users.
3. **Facilitate:** Build consensus and commitment by making users easier to understand as real people.
4. **Measure:** Provide the means for measuring the design’s effectiveness.
5. **Support Ancillary Efforts:** Contribute to efforts like marketing and sales plans.

- The fundamental goal is to successfully accommodate a variety of users by designing for **specific types of individuals with specific needs**, rather than designing broad functionality that satisfies nobody (avoiding a "confusing potpourri").
- Personas engage the **empathy** of the design and development team around the users’ goals.

**C. Issues Personas Address (Problems in Design)**

- Designing software to please too many users often results in low user satisfaction.
- **Three key issues that arise during development:**
    1. **The elastic user:** Users could be assumed to be computer literate or not, or beginner, intermediate, or expert. One designer’s view of the user may not match that of others.
    2. **Self-referential design:** Designers project their own goals, motivations, skills, and mental models onto the design, often designing with only functionality in mind and making untested presumptions about the target user.
    3. **Edge cases:** Developers design for highly specific scenarios that may or may not ever occur, or cases that real users would not create.
