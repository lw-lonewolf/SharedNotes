**A. User Goals and Cognitive Processing (Norman’s Model)**

- Understanding personas centers on **motivations and goals**, rather than specific tasks or demographics.
- Goals are the **drivers behind the behaviors**.
- The product function must address goals via tasks, completed in as few tasks as possible.
- The linking of persona goals to Norman’s model defines three top-level user motivations:

| Goal Type            | Cognitive Processing Level             | Focus / Driver                                                        | Importance and Examples                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| :------------------- | :------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Experience Goals** | **Visceral** (How user wants to feel)  | The user's reaction to a product’s visual and sensory aspects.        | **Most important level of processing**. Users initially judge attractive interfaces as more usable. Examples: To feel smart and in control, to have fun, to feel reassured about security, to remain focused and alert.                                           |
| **End Goals**        | **Behavioral** (What user wants to do) | The desired outcome in mind when using the product.                   | This level should be the **primary focus of design efforts**. End goals must be met for users to think the product is worth their time and money. Examples: Clear my daily to-do list, maintain connectedness, awareness of problems before they become critical. |
| **Life Goals**       | **Reflective** (Who user wants to be)  | The user's long-term desires, motivations, and self-image attributes. | Transcends the context of the specific product design. Related to designing for long-term product relationships.                                                                                                                                                  |

- Making users feel stupid (by letting them make big mistakes, preventing them from getting adequate work done, or boring them) should be avoided.

**B. Other Goal Types**

- **Business & Organizational Goals:** What the product is expected to accomplish (e.g., increase profits, increase market share, retain customers, defeat competition, secure IP).
- **Customer Goals:** Goals of customers (parents, relatives, friends) in relation to the product; these goals should **never override the user’s "end goals"**.
- **Technical Goals:** Goals related to the ease of SW creation, maintenance, scalability, extensibility, data integrity, execution efficiency, and consistency across platforms.
