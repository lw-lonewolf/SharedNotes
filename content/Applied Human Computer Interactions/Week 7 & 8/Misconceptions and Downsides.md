**A. Common Misconceptions**

- **Misconception 1: Personas are made up.** Behavior patterns should be real and derived from actual ethnographic data. The fictional names and pictures lead to this misconception.
- **Misconception 2: Personas aren't as useful as involving real people.** Focusing on an individual persona (derived from aggregated behavior sets) is less likely to miss key behaviors than focusing solely on aggregated real users.
- **Misconception 3: Users think in terms of tasks.** User goals are the focus; users don't think in terms of tasks.

**B. Downsides/Risks**

1. Focusing on the wrong design target.
2. Focusing on the right target but missing key behaviors that could differentiate the product.
3. Difficulty getting buy-in from individuals/groups who did not participate in their creation.
4. Discrediting the value of personas, causing the organization to reject their use long-term.

**C. Provisional Personas (When Field Data is Missing)** If working without field data, it is important to take steps to mitigate risks:

- Clearly label and explain them as provisional.
- Represent them visually with **sketches, not photos**, to reinforce their provisional nature.
- Use as much existing data as possible (market surveys, domain research, SMEs, etc.).
- Document what data was used and what assumptions were made.
- Steer clear of stereotypes (which is more difficult without field data).
- Focus on behaviors and goals, not demographics.
