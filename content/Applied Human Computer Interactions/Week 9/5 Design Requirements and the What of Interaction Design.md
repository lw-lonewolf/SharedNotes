Design requirements represent **the "What" of Interaction Design**—the information and capabilities the personas require to accomplish their goals. Requirements are synonymous with **needs**.

#### A. Needs vs. Solutions (Requirements vs. Features)

It is crucial that requirements are **NOT features**. They are also not a list of capabilities generated by others (i.e., not users).

- **Consequence of Mandating Solutions:** When solutions are mandated _before_ the design process (as often happens in Marketing Requirements Documents (MRDs) and Product Requirements Documents (PRDs) that confuse _what_ the product should do with _how_), the result is "clunky and disjointed interactions and products".
    
- **The Power of Defining Needs:** Starting with requirements (needs) rather than solutions gives interaction designers the ability to create powerful and compelling products. By defining user needs clearly, designers enable technologists to find the best viable and feasible solutions without compromising the product’s ability to help people achieve their goals.
    
- **Detailed Example (Data Analytics Tool):** Without understanding the fundamental "what" (the underlying needs), the focus might be placed merely on generating reports, leading to a huge volume of reports being cataloged during user research. However, the real requirement might be providing a way to **“recognize exceptional situations before opportunities are missed or problems arise”**.
    

#### B. Deriving and Structuring Requirements

Requirements are extracted by **analyzing the approved context scenario**.

- **Structure:** Design requirements typically consist of **objects, actions, and contexts**.
- _Requirement Example (based on Vivien's scenario):_ **Call (action) a person (object) directly for an appointment (context)**.

#### C. Other Types of Requirements

While design requirements focus on user needs, other constraints must also be defined:

- **Business Requirements:** These encompass stakeholder priorities, development timelines, budgetary and resource constraints, regulations and legal considerations, pricing structures, and business models.
- **Brand and Experience Requirements:** These reflect the attributes (e.g., emotional tone, visual style) that the team wants users and customers to associate with the product, company, or organization.
- **Technical Requirements:** These involve constraints such as weight, size, form factor, display, power constraints, and software platform choices.
- **Customer and Partner Requirements:** These can include factors like ease of installation.
