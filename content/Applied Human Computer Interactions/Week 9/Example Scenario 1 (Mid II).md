**Scenario:**  
You are part of a design team working on an **online learning platform** aimed at university students who want flexible study schedules. The current goal is to improve **student engagement and course completion rates**.

**Questions:**  
a) Define the **primary persona** and **secondary persona** for this platform. Include details such as **user role**, **market segment**, and **motivations**.  
b) Using the **five-step requirements definition process**, list the major **design requirements** you would identify for improving engagement.  
c) Discuss the **goals and motivations** that would guide your persona design — how would they affect interface decisions?  
d) Construct a short **narrative or scenario** describing how your primary persona interacts with the course progress tracker feature.  
e) Explain how this case shows the **“what” of interaction design**, not the “how.”

#### (A)
Primary Persona: Ali, a full-time student,academia, aims to maximize learning and productivity, will use this online learning platform on a daily basis 
Secondary Persona: Umar, a student counselor, aims to track performance efficiently and quickly,  who uses the app occasionally to observe performance and other metrics

#### (B)
1) Research: Circulate questionnaires among students and arrange interviews with the faculty 
2) Identify Behavior Patterns: Notice patterns that the students follow along with their reception/repulsion towards proposed features.
3) Synthesize Characteristics: Merge shared traits/behaviors to form one representative persona with similar goals and frustrations
4) Define Goals: Set the persona's goals around efficiency, productivity and convenience
5) Validate and Share with team 

#### (C) 
Important motivations for this design would involve efficiency and time-saving mechanisms, convenience and ease of access. The user's goals such as "attempt online self-testing activities", "view grades", "view student perfomances" will play an important role in designing the interface. e.g. in this case we'll aim for a simple and fluent design that is intuitive and does not waste the user's time while navigating to a feature. 

#### (D)
Ali opens the platform, logs himsef in and selects the "Check Progress" option from the navigation bar in order to view his current progress presented in the form of quantitative metrics as well as visual representations.

#### (E)
In this case we're focusing on "what" the main persona, i.e. Ali requires from the app and how we can minimize effort required to achieve his goal instead of focusing on the exact solution i.e. how he'll do it  




## MODEL  ANSWER
**(A) Personas**  
**Primary — Ali, 21, CS undergrad (daily user):** full-time student who studies evenings. Goal: complete modules on schedule and reduce time wasted finding what to study next. Frustration: long lectures, unclear progress. Quote: “I want a one-glance plan for today.”  
**Secondary — Umar, 35, Student Counselor (weekly user):** monitors cohort progress, spots at-risk students, exports reports. Goal: quickly identify & support disengaged students.

**(B) Five-step requirements → major design requirements**

1. Identify stakeholders & goals (students, instructors, counselors; increase engagement/completion).
2. Gather data (surveys, analytics, interviews).
3. Derive requirements:
- R1 Progress dashboard with next action (High)
- R2 Micro-learning modules (High)    
- R3 Smart reminders + calendar sync (High)
- R4 Instant feedback & low-stakes quizzes (High)
- R5 Goal setting & milestone badges (Medium)
- R6 Counselor alerts & exportable reports (Medium)
- R7 Fast mobile UI + offline sync (Non-functional, High)
4. Prioritize 
5. Validate with students and analytics.
**(C) Goals & motivations → interface effects**

- **Efficiency/time saving** → one-tap “Resume” and a scannable progress card showing next action.
- **Convenience** → responsive mobile layout, offline downloads.
- **Mastery/accountability** → immediate quiz feedback, milestones, calendar reminders.  
    These drive minimal navigation depth, clear visual hierarchy, and micro-interactions for quick wins.

**(D) Narrative (key scenario)**  
Ali logs in → taps “Progress” → dashboard shows 62% complete, “Next: Module 5 quiz — 8 min,” and a suggested 10-min micro-lesson. He taps “Resume,” completes the micro-lesson and a 5-question quiz, gets instant feedback (3/5), system updates progress to 64% and schedules a revision reminder for 19:00 tomorrow.

**(E) “What” vs “How”**  
We describe **what** the user needs (clear progress, next actions, reminders, short lessons). The **how** (specific UI widgets, backend sync, frameworks) is implementation detail to be designed after requirements validation.
