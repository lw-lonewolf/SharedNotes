The ultimate goal of this process is to create design solutions that **satisfy and inspire users** by drawing on the understanding of people.

#### A. The Design Process Framework

Interaction design is defined primarily as **the design of behavior that occurs over time**. The overall process of bridging the gap between research and design is structured in four steps, with **personas** serving as the main characters:

1. **Developing stories or scenarios:** This involves imagining ideal user interactions.
2. **Extracting design requirements:** Requirements are drawn directly frotive of a "day in the life".
    - Include: Behaviors (activities/motivations), user environment, frustrations/pain points, relevant demographics, skills/experience, attitudes/emotions, and alternate or competing ways of doing the same thing.
    - The authors strongly recommend the scenarios developed in step one.
3. **Defining the interaction framework:** These requirements are used to define the product’s fundamental interaction framework.
4. **Adding detail:** The final step involves filling in that framework with ever-increasing amounts of detail.

#### B. The Power of Narrative (Scenarios)

The use of narrative in design leverages creativity significantly:

- Imagining a story about a person using the product **leverages our creativity to a greater power** than simply imagining a better form factor or configuration of screen elements.
- Experiences designed around a narrative (a story) tend to be **more comprehensible and engaging for users**.
- _Example of Form Factor:_ The form factor refers to the physical or digital container for the product. Examples include a web application viewed on a high-resolution computer screen, a phone that must be small, light, and low-resolution for use in both bright sunlight and dark, or a kiosk.
