Scenarios are foundational to interaction design, helping define the behavior, priority, and presentation of product functions. John Carroll characterizes scenarios as **"paradoxically concrete but rough, tangible but flexible"**.

#### A. Characteristics and Value

- Scenarios implicitly encourage **"what-if?" thinking** among all parties involved.
- They allow for the articulation of design possibilities **without undermining innovation**.
- They compel attention to the **use that will be made of the design product**.
- Scenarios can describe situations at many levels of detail, helping to coordinate various aspects of the design project.
- Goal-Directed scenarios are an **iterative means** of defining the product's behavior from the standpoint of specific users (personas). This includes the functionality, the priority of functions, and the way those functions are expressed in terms of the user interface.

#### B. Three Types of Scenarios

| Scenario Type                                         | Timing & Level                                                                                | Focus and Application Details                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                               |
| :---------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Context Scenarios**                              | High-level description created _before_ any design is performed. This is where design begins. | Focus is on human activities, perceptions, and desires, written from the persona's perspective. They tell the story of a particular persona using the future product in the most typical way. The focus is on **how the product can best help personas achieve their goals**. High-level actions are described from the user's perspective. |
| **2. Key Path Scenarios** (Revised Context Scenarios) | Less detailed than context scenarios.                                                         | Incorporate functional and data elements and the Design Framework. They typically take the form of a number of **“what if . . .” questions** about the proposed solutions.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **3. Validation Scenarios**                           | Used in later stages.                                                                         | Used by the team to **test the design solution**.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

#### C. Application Example: Vivien’s Context Scenario

The context scenario for Vivien, a primary persona for a personal digital device (PDA), illustrates high-level usage and goal-directed actions:

1. Vivien, while getting ready, uses her phone to check email because it has a large enough screen and quick connection time, making it more convenient than booting a computer, especially as she rushes to make her daughter a sandwich.
2. Vivien sees an email from her newest client, Frank, who wants to see a house that afternoon.
3. Since the device has Frank’s contact information, she can **call him with a simple action right from the email**.
    - _Design Guidance:_ Designers should notice how the activities in the scenario tie back to Vivien's goals and try to eliminate as many unnecessary tasks as possible.
