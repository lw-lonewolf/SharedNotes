This process aims to answer broad questions about **what a product is and what it should do**. Steps 3 through 5 are cycled through repeatedly until the requirements are stable.

1. **Create problem and vision statements**:
    
    - **Mandate and Consensus:** These statements provide a clear mandate for moving forward and are extremely helpful in building consensus among stakeholders.
    - **Stakeholder Buy-in:** It is critical to connect business issues to usability issues to drive stakeholder buy-in and frame the design effort in terms of both user and business goals.
    - _Sample Template:_ The new design of Product X will help users achieve G by allowing them to do X, Y and Z with greater [accuracy, efficiency, and so on], and without problems A, B and C that they currently experience. This will dramatically improve Company X's customer satisfaction ratings and lead to increased market share.
2. **Explore / brainstorm**:
    
    - **Goal:** The primary purpose is to **eliminate as much preconception as possible**. This allows designers to be open-minded and flexible.
    - **Activity:** Focus is on how the personas would likely want to engage with the product. Brainstorming is used to record ideas and "let them go" for the time being. This step must be highly collaborative.
    - **Mental Model vs. Implementation Model:** The represented model of the interfaces (how the design behaves and presents itself) should match what the team understands about the **users‘ mental model** as much as possible. The represented model should **not reflect the implementation model** (how the product will actually be constructed internally).
3. **Identify persona expectations**:
    
    - For each persona (primary and secondary), designers must identify:
        - Attitudes, experiences, aspirations, and other social, cultural, environmental, and cognitive factors that influence their expectations.
        - General expectations and desires about the product experience.
        - Behaviors the persona will expect or want from the product.
        - How that persona thinks about basic elements or units of data (e.g., in an email application, data elements might be messages and people).
    - **Clues from Interviews:** Pay close attention to: what interview subjects mention first; the action words (verbs) and nouns they use; and which intermediate steps, tasks, or objects in a process they **do not mention** (these might not be important to their thinking).
