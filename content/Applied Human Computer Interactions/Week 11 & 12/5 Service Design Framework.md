
#### 1. Describe Customer Journeys

This phase involves creating a **descriptive narrative** that details how a persona interacts with the service. The journey is documented from the **first exposure** all the way through to the **final transaction**. These narratives allow designers to identify **secondary paths**, which are essential for helping a persona recover from **nuanced problems** they might encounter during the process.

#### 2. Create a Service Blueprint

A service blueprint offers a **"big picture"** view of the service. It identifies every **touch point** where a persona interacts with the service and describes the **"backstage" processes** that must occur to deliver that service effectively. By focusing on the customer experience first, designers can often uncover **unexpected touch points** that might have been missed in a purely operational map.

#### 3. Create Experience Prototypes

Service designers use **experience prototypes** to illustrate a persona’s individual interaction with the service. These prototypes often include **mock-ups of key touch points** to provide a tangible representation of how the service will function in a real-world scenario.

---

**Analogy for Understanding:** Defining a service design framework is like **orchestrating a play**. The **customer journey** is the script, detailing the main character's path from the opening scene to the finale. The **service blueprint** is the stage manager's guide, showing not only what the audience sees (the touch points) but also what happens behind the curtains to move the scenery (the backstage processes). Finally, the **experience prototype** is the dress rehearsal, where the actors use mock props to ensure the entire performance flows smoothly before the actual opening night.
