Statistics is the branch of maths that deals with the collection, organization, analysis, interpretation and presentation of numerical data. It helps in making decisions based on data and is used almost in every field including business, economics, medicine, and engineering.

#### **Characteristics of Statistics**

1. **Aggregates of facts** – Statistics deals with a group of facts rather than an individual observation.
2. **Numerical representation** – Data in statistics must be expressed in numerical form.
3. **Comparison and classification** – Data is analyzed by comparing different sets and categorizing them.
4. **Affected by multiplicity of causes** – Statistical results are influenced by multiple factors.
5. **Deals with variability** – Statistics accounts for variations in data.
6. **Used for prediction** – Statistical methods help predict future trends based on past data.
#### **Uses of Statistics**

- Helps in **decision-making** for businesses and governments.
- Supports **scientific research** by analyzing experimental data.
- Provides **insights in economics**, such as inflation and GDP analysis.
- Assists in **quality control** in manufacturing industries.
- Aids in **medical studies** for testing drugs and treatments.

### **2. Study Designs**

Study designs describe how data is collected and analyzed in statistics.

#### **Cross-sectional Study**

- Observes data **at a single point in time**.
- Useful for understanding the **current state** of a population.
- Example: A survey conducted to study the smoking habits of university students.

#### **Time Series Study**

- Observes data **over multiple time periods**.
- Used for studying trends and forecasting future data.
- Example: Analyzing the sales of a company over the last 10 years.

**Key Difference:**  
Cross-sectional studies capture a **snapshot** of data at a particular time, while time series studies analyze **changes over time**.

### **3. Errors in Statistics**

Errors occur in statistical data collection and can affect results.

#### **Systematic Errors**

- Occur due to consistent flaws in measurement or data collection.
- Can be caused by faulty equipment, biased sampling, or incorrect methods.
- Example: A scale that always adds 2 kg to a person’s weight.

#### **Random Errors**

- Occur unpredictably and are caused by unknown factors.
- Can be reduced by increasing the sample size.
- Example: Fluctuations in temperature affecting the measurement of liquids.

#### **Sampling Errors**

- Arise when a sample does not accurately represent the population.
- Can occur due to biased sampling techniques.
- Example: Surveying only urban students to study education levels in an entire country.

### **4. Branches of Statistics**

Statistics is divided into two main branches:

#### **Descriptive Statistics**

- Deals with **summarizing and organizing** data.
- Uses measures such as **mean, median, mode, standard deviation, and graphs**.
- Example: Calculating the **average height** of students in a class.

#### **Inferential Statistics**

- Uses **sample data** to make conclusions about a **population**.
- Involves hypothesis testing, confidence intervals, and probability distributions.
- Example: Estimating the **percentage of people** who prefer a particular brand based on a survey.

**Key Difference:**  
Descriptive statistics **describe** data, while inferential statistics **draw conclusions** from data.

### **5. Sample and Population**

#### **Population**

- The **entire group** that a study is focused on.
- Example: All students in a university.

#### **Sample**

- A **subset** of the population selected for study.
- Example: 200 students selected from a university for a survey.

**Why use a sample?**

- Studying an entire population is **time-consuming and costly**.
- A well-chosen sample provides **accurate estimations**.

### **6. Grouped and Ungrouped Data**

#### **Ungrouped Data**

- Data that is **not categorized** into groups.
- Example: Raw exam scores of students: **45, 78, 67, 90, 56**

#### **Grouped Data**

- Data is **organized into categories or intervals**.
- Example:
    
    |Score Range|Number of Students|
    |---|---|
    |40 - 50|2|
    |50 - 60|3|
    |60 - 70|4|
    

**Key Difference:**  
Ungrouped data is raw, while grouped data is **organized into classes** for easier analysis.

### **7. Dataset**

A **dataset** is a structured collection of data used for statistical analysis.

#### **Components of a dataset:**

- **Variables:** Characteristics being measured (e.g., age, income).
- **Observations:** Individual records in the dataset.
- **Values:** Specific data points in the dataset.

Example of a dataset:

| Student ID | Age | GPA |
| ---------- | --- | --- |
| 101        | 20  | 3.5 |
| 102        | 22  | 3.8 |
### **8. Types of Variables**

#### **A. Qualitative vs. Quantitative Variables**

- **Qualitative (Categorical) Variables**: Describe qualities or characteristics.
    - Example: Gender (Male/Female), Eye Color (Blue, Brown).
- **Quantitative (Numerical) Variables**: Represent measurable quantities.
    - Example: Age (in years), Salary (in dollars).

#### **B. Discrete vs. Continuous Variables**

- **Discrete Variables**: Can take **only specific values** (countable).
    - Example: Number of students in a class (1, 2, 3, …).
- **Continuous Variables**: Can take **any value within a range** (measurable).
    - Example: Height (e.g., 5.4 ft, 5.6 ft).
