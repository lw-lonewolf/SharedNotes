The *humanistic* model was developed by Abraham Maslow and Carl Rogers as a response to the theories of Psychoanalysis and Behaviorism, this approach emphasizes personal responsibility, free will, inherent goodness of individuals. It focuses on human potential and individual growth, and the pursuit of self-actualization. 

#### Key Assumptions: 
* A *healthy mental attitude* depends on: 
	* Taking personal responsibility
	* Recognizing the extent of free will, meaning individuals have the power to change their own lives.
	* Striving towards personal growth and fulfillment, which leads to a sense of purpose and self-actualization.

#### Self Actualization: 
According to Abraham Maslow, people have the innate drive to *self-actualization*, which is the process of realizing their full potential and becoming the best version of themselves, Maslows *Heirarchy of Need* outlines how individuals must first fulfill basic needs (like food, safety, love) before they can focus on self-actualization. 

#### Carl Roger's Person-Centered Therapy: 
Carl Rogers emphasized the importance of an environment that fosters growth and self actualization. One key element in this is unconditioned positive regard, which means accepting and valuing a person without any condition or judgement. 

Rogers' argument was that when children receive that, they develop healthily and move toward self-actualization. On the other hand, children that have conditions of worth imposed on them (for example they must attain a certain level of academic excellence to be praised or loved), they may develop insecurities and fail to reach their full potential.

#### Defensive Thinking: 
* According to Rogers, people sometimes use *Defensive Thinking* to protect their self-potential. For example some people use rationalization, to justify or morph their real motives or actions to align with how they view themselves. This defense mechanism prevents them from fully understanding their feelings or motives and hinders growth.
