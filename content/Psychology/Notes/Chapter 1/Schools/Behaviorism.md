**John B Watson**

This emerged in the early 20th Century with key figures likes John B Watson and B.F. Skinner, building on the work of Ivan Pavlov.

Behaviorism is a theoretical orientation based on the premise that psychology should study only observable behavior. Watson's argument was that all behavior is the result of shaping / conditioning of the environment. He denied that innate or inborn behavioral tendencies could exist. He denied the influence of genetics. 
##### Nature vs Nurture:
John leaned towards nurture rather than nature. He believed that behaviors are determined by the environment and the beings interaction with it rather than genetics etc. 
##### Stimulus Organism Response Model:
Behaviorism operates on the SOR framework, where a stimulus from the environment prompts a response from the organism. The "O" stands for Organism, but Watson deemphasized internal mental processes and instead focused on the observable stimulus and response.
##### Ivan Pavlov
He built upon *Ivan Pavlov's* findings, where he found out that in dogs, a certain stimuli "ringing of a bell" could become associated with an unconditioned stimulus "food" in order to incite a reaction "salivation". This had influence on Watson's theory. 
##### B. F Skinner
Later BF Skinner expanded on Behaviorism through his concept of *operant conditioning*, where behavior is controlled through rewards and punishments. He believed that free will is an illusion, suggest that all behavior is determined by external factors and reinforcement history rather than decision making.

`As a summary Behaviorism sees behavior as a product of learning and environment, downplaying innate tendencies or internal states, and focuses heavily on conditioning processes.`
