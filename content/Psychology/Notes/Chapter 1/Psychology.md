##### Literal Definition: 
The word Psychology is derived from two Greek words *psyche* and *logos*. 
*psyche* means "related to the soul" and *logos* which means "the study of something". These two greek roots were first put together in the 18th Century to define soul, spirit, or mind, as distinguished from the body (Boring, 1966). It was not until the late 18th century did the term *psychology* gain more then rare usage among scholars. By that time it had gained the meaning "the study of the mind".
##### Modern Definition: 
Psychology is the scientific study of behavior and mental processes. Behavior is observable and is quantifiable (i.e. it can be counted.). The problem is that in order to understand humans they do a lot more then behave. They may be physically present and mentally absent etc. Some people have different mental states and capacities. Behavior may be controlled, thoughts can not. Thoughts that are thrown under the rug or suppressed always bounce back with a high enough magnitude.
##### Is Psychology a Science:
Psychology is indeed a science because it follows the empirical method. Empiricism emphasizes objective and precise measurements. Psychology and the other behavioral or social sciences follow this method, although they are not as accurate or precise as other sciences like Physics, Chemistry etc. but they are precise enough to be treated as a science, also their findings can be referred to as scientific. 
##### Goals of Psychology: 
Observation, description, explanation and prediction of behavior through systematic methodology.
##### Brief Intro to the Birth of Psychology:
Psychology's intellectual parents were the branches of philosophy and physiology, by the 1870s a small number of scholars in both field were looking into questions about the mind. How are bodily suggestions turned into mental awareness of the outside world? Are people's perceptions of the world accurate reflections of the reality? How does the mind and the brain interact? The thing was these scholars were interested in only its effects *within* their respective fields. It was a German Professor `Wilhelm Wundt` (1832-1920) who first changed the this view. Wundt mounted a campaign to make psychology an independent discipline rather then a stepchild of psychology or physiology. The time and place were right, the academic community of Germany welcomed his appeal and the conducive intellectual environment of Germany also played an important role. in 1879, Wundt established the first formal lab for research in psychology at University of Leipzig. This is why 1879 has been christened as the `date of birth` of psychology.
