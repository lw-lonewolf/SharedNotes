*Attitude:*
1) It refers to a set of emotions, beliefs or behaviors toward a particular object, person, thing or event. 
2) Attitude may be positive or negative evaluations of objects of thought. 
3) An attitude is a general and lasting positive or negative opinion or feeling about some person, object or issue.

#### Components of Attitude 
The components of attitude are sometimes referred to as ABC 
A: *Affective Component:* How the object, person, issue, or event makes you feel.
B: *Behavioral Component:* How attitude influences your behavior
C: *Cognitive Component:* Your thoughts and beliefs about the subject.

#### How Attitudes Form 
The factors which influence and affect formation of attitudes are: 
-> Experience: *direct personal experience, or they may result from observation.*
-> Social Factors: *how people are expected to behave in a particular role or context*
-> Learning
-> Conditioning: e.g. Negative/Positive feedback on activities
-> Observational Learning

#### Theories about attitude changes 
##### Learning Theory: 
`Classical Conditioning`, `Operant Conditioning`, `Observational Learning` can be used to bring about attitude change. 

*Classical:* will change <u>emotional reactions</u> to an object. e.g. ads using stimuli like highly attractive models or likable spokespersons to pair product with positive emotional reactions.

*Operational:* Openly express your viewpoint. People agree (reward -> increases this attitude), people disagree (punishment -> attitude decreases)

*Observational Learning:*
e.g. opinion/attitudes/behaviors of people like teachers/co-workers/parents/coaches etc.

##### Persuasion Theory (Elaboration Likelihood Model)

suggests attitudes can be altered in one of two ways. The *central route* -> motivated to listen and think about the message (content) leading to an attitude shift. 
*peripheral route* -> people might be influenced by characteristics of the speaker -> temporary/surface shift in attitude. 

>[!NOTE]
>*MESSAGES THAT ARE THOUGHT PROVOKING AND THAT FOLLOW LOGIC (central route)  ARE MOST LIKELY TO LEAD TO PERMANENT ATTITUDE CHANGES*

__CENTRAL ROUTE__
```
A car company launches a new electric vehicle. Their marketing
campaign focuses on detailed specifications: range, charging time, safety features, efficiency ratings, and independent test results showcasing its performance and reliability. They highlight the environmental benefits with factual data on emissions reduction compared to gasoline vehicles. Consumers who are persuaded by this campaign are engaging in central route processing; they are carefully weighing the evidence presented to decide if the car is a good purchase for them. The persuasion relies on the inherent quality and logic of the information presented.
```
__PERIPHERAL ROUTE__
```
The same car company also runs a commercial featuring a popular
celebrity driving the electric vehicle through scenic landscapes,
accompanied by upbeat music.  The commercial doesn't focus on technical details; instead, it creates a feeling of excitement and aspiration. Viewers who are persuaded by this commercial are engaging in peripheral route processing.  They are associating positive feelings (linked to the celebrity and the imagery) with the car, without necessarily deeply evaluating its practical features.  The persuasion hinges on peripheral cues (celebrity endorsement, appealing visuals) rather than the car's actual specifications.
```

##### Cognitive Dissonance Theory
Dissonance leads to changed behaviors. This means when there is a conflict in beliefs about a topic, and both can not coexist at the same time, people will shift their attitudes. 
* Was First Studied by *Leon Fostinger*
* People want to maintain consistency in beliefs and behaviors 
* Refers to an imbalance in what we believe (cognitive) or what we do (conation). This can cause severe psychological discomfort 
* Hence, either we set the *imbalance* by either changing our beliefs
```
The information regarding smoking with cancer and heart 
disease create dissonance in chain smokers. They cant resist the 
temptation of smoking yet, knowing smoking is injurious to health. There is an imbalance between the beliefs and smoking behavior. The remedy lies in a goal directed behavior that is aimed to reduce the imbalance either by stopping excessive smoking or by refusing to believe the information about its danger.
```
