The Trial and Error technique of learning refers to solving a problem by attempting various approaches and learning from mistakes until a solution is found. It's a process in which animals try different responses to a problem and eliminate those that fail until they reach a response that succeeds. 

### Historical Context: Edward Thorndike's Experiments

The concept of trial and error was popularized by **Edward Thorndike**, a psychologist known for his work with animal behavior in the early 20th century. Thorndike conducted experiments with cats placed in a "puzzle box." The cats had to figure out how to escape the box by pulling a lever or performing other actions.

- **Law of Effect**: Thorndike's experiments led to his formulation of the **Law of Effect**, which states that behaviors followed by satisfying outcomes are more likely to be repeated, while those followed by negative outcomes are less likely to be repeated. This is a key principle in operant conditioning and explains why trial and error learning works.
