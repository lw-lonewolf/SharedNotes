This is a change in knowledge due to experience. This is about cognitive abilities like memory systems (code, store, recall, decode)

In another variation of the experiment, Tolman found that once rats learned the layout of a maze (their **cognitive map**), they could find a shortcut to the goal when the usual route was blocked. This demonstrated that the rats weren’t just following learned behaviors through trial and error but were actively using their internal representation of the maze to find new routes.
### Tolman’s Experiments: Cognitive Maps and Latent Learning

#### 1. **The Maze Experiment**:

Tolman’s most famous experiment involved rats navigating mazes. He conducted a study where rats were placed in a maze and given time to explore without receiving any immediate rewards.

- **Experiment Setup**: Tolman had three groups of rats:
    
    - **Group 1**: Rats received food at the end of the maze from the start.
    - **Group 2**: Rats never received food.
    - **Group 3**: Rats didn’t receive food initially, but after a certain number of days, they started receiving food rewards at the end of the maze.
- **Findings**:
    
    - Rats in Group 1 quickly learned to navigate the maze to find food.
    - Rats in Group 2 wandered aimlessly without learning the maze.
    - Interestingly, rats in Group 3, who initially received no food, wandered in the maze much like Group 2. However, once they started receiving food (on day 11), their learning suddenly improved drastically, almost as if they had learned the layout of the maze during the previous days but hadn’t demonstrated it until they were motivated to do so.

This demonstrated **latent learning**, where the rats had learned the maze even without reinforcement and only exhibited that learning when food was introduced as an incentive.
