The acquisition of information, skills, or behavior through watching the performance of others is called Observation learning. This is also called `vicarous learning`. This behavior is most common in children where they observe and then try to imitate behaviors of adults. 

### Bandura's Bobo Doll Experiment:

One of the most famous studies on observational learning was conducted by **Albert Bandura** in the 1960s. In this experiment, children watched adults interact with a large inflatable doll (the **Bobo doll**). The adults either acted aggressively (hitting and kicking the doll) or non-aggressively.

- **Findings**: The children who observed aggressive behavior were more likely to act aggressively toward the doll themselves. This demonstrated that children learn and replicate behaviors they observe, even without any direct reinforcement.
    
- **Vicarious Reinforcement**: The children were more likely to imitate the model's behavior if they saw the model being rewarded for it, which is a form of **vicarious reinforcement**. Conversely, if the model was punished, the children were less likely to imitate the behavior.
