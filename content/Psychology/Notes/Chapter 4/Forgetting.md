# HERMAN EBBINGHAUS 
* first to conduct scientific studies on forgetting, published work in 1885
* used nonsense syllables to avoid previous learning influences (e.g BAF, XOF). 
* Forgetting curve: Ebbinghaus's forgetting curve showed that forgetting happens most frequently right after learning new information.
* Subsequent Research showed that this was most likely due to the nonsense syllables he used, forgetting curves for nonsense materials are more steeper then other material, prose/poetry for example.
# Retention Techniques 
-> Recall Technique: Requires subject to reproduce information on their own without any cues. e.g. essay questions etc. 
-> Recognition: Requires subject to select previously learned information form an array of options. e.g. MCQs 
-> Relearning: Requires a subject to relearn or memorize information a second time to determine how much time or how many practice trials are saved by having learned it before. 

# Theories of Forgetting
-> Decay Theory
-> Interference Theory: 
	-> Proactive (Interference of previously learned shit, on to new crap)
	-> Retroactive (Interference of new information, on already learned information)
-> Motivated Forgetting (Repression)
	bad memories etc. buried into subconscious.
-> Cue Dependent Forgetting:
	Forgetting something, because of lack of retrieval cue (forgot keys in library, if you remember you were in library you will remember you left keys on the table, otherwise you wont)

# Memory Impairments
*Amnesia:* Memory loss that occurs without other mental difficulties (head injuries, certain drugs, alcohol)
*Retrograde Amnesia:* Forgetting events before a certain time. 
*Anterograde Amnesia:* Forgetting events after a certain event or time.
*Alzheimer's Disease:* A mental disease that results in degradation of brain and cognitive functions over time.
*Korsakoff's Syndrome:* A disease that afflicts long-term alcoholics, leaving some abilities intact but including hallucinations or tendencies to repeat or relapse.
