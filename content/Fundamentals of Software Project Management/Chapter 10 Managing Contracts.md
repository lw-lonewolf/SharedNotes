# Types of Contracts 
## On Basis of System Type
A contract for a *completed software package* can be placed. This could be for one of the following types of system: 
* A ***Bespoke System***: Created specifically for the one customer.
* An ***Off-the-Shelf Package***: bought 'as is'.
* ***Customized Off-the-Shelf (COTS)***: This software - where a core system is modified to meet the needs of the client.

>[!important] Legality of Ownership
>With the supply of software a contract may be regarded as supplying a service (i.e. to write the software) or the granting of a *licence* (i.e. permission) to use the software which in turns remains under the ownership of the supplier

## On Basis of Payment 
* ***Fixed Price Contracts***:
	A price is fixed when the contract is signed. If there are no changes in the contract terms, this is the price that they shall pay on completion. For this to be effective the customer's requirements must be fixed in the beginning.
	
	**Advantages:**
	 * As long as requirements are precise and unchanged, the customer has a known cost.
	 * The supplier has a motivation to work in a cost-effective manner.
	
	**Disadvantages:**
	 * The supplier absorbs the risk for any errors in the estimates 
	 * The need to change the scope of requirements may become apparent later during development
	 * When competing against other potential suppliers, the supplier will try to quote as low of a price as possible.
	 * The need to meet a fixed price means quality of the software would suffer
* ***Time and Materials Contracts:***
	The customer is charged at a fixed rate per unit of effort, for example per staff-hour. The supplier may provide an initial cost estimate based on their understanding of the customer's requirements but this is not basis for final payment. As this appears to be a blank cheque for the 
	
	**Advantages:**
	* When a project has a research orientation and requirements may change as options are explored, then this may be an appropriate method of payment.
	* The lack of price pressure may promote better quality deliverables.
	
	**Disadvantages:**
	* Customer absorbs the risk with poorly defined or changing requirements.
	* The supplier has no incentive to work in a cost-effective manner.
* ***Fixed Price Per Unit Delivered:***
	Often associated with FP counting. The size of the system to be delivered is calculated or estimated at the outset of the project. The size could be estimated in lines of code, but FPs can be more easily derived from requirements documents. A price per unit is also quoted.The final price is then $\text{Unit Price} \times \text{Number of Units}$.
	
	**Advantages:**
	* Customer knows how price is calculated and how it will vary with changing requirements.
	* Pricing schedules can be compared
	* The supplier does not bear risk of increasing functionality 
	* The supplier still has incentive to deliver the required functionality in a cost-effective manner
	
	**Disadvantages:**
	* Difficulties with software size measurement. Line codes can be inflated using a verbose coding style, on the other hand FP counting rules may be seen as unfairly favoring either the supplier or the consumer.
	* Some requested changes may affect existing code drastically but not increase overall FP count. A change made too late in the cycle will usually require more effort to implement.

## On basis of Tender Type 
### Open Tendering Process
Any supplier can bid to supply the goods and services. All bids compliant with the original conditions in the invitation to tender must be considered and evaluated in the same way. With a major project this can be time consuming and expensive.
### Restricted Tendering
In this case, tenders are only from suppliers who have been invited by the customer. Unlike open tendering, the customer may at any point reduce number of potential suppliers. Usually, this is the best approach.
### Negotiated Procedure
In this case, only a certain supplier is invited for a tender. This however exposes customer to charges of favoritism and should only be done with clear justifications.

# Stages of Contract Placement
## Requirements Analysis
Before approaching potential suppliers, you need to have a clear set of requirements. In this situation, an external consultant can also be approached to draw up a requirements document. 

`The lack of, or defects in, the specification are probably the heart of most disputes resulting from the acquisition of computer equipment and software`
***David Bainbridge***
### Main Sections of a Requirements Document
* Introduction
* Description of Existing Systems and Current Environment
* Customer's future strategy or plans 
* System Requirements
	* Mandatory
	* Desirable
* Deadlines 
* Additional information required from potential suppliers

>[!info] Mandatory vs. Desirable System Requirements
>***Mandatory:*** If a proposal does not meet this requirement, this is grounds for immediate rejection
>***Desirable:*** A proposal might be deficient in this respect, but other features of the proposal would compensate for this.

## Evaluation Plan
Now that we have a list of requirements, the next part is a plan of how the proposals are to be evaluated. Ways of checking if mandatory requirements are met need to be identified. 
## Invitation to Tender 
Now that we have both a requirements document and a plan to evaluate proposals, it is now possible to issue the invitation to tender to suppliers. 
This ITT will contain: 
* Requirements Document
* Supporting Letter with info on Lodging
* Deadline

>[!important] Two Stage ITT
>Sometimes, for complex systems we have two stages. During the ***first stage***, technical proposals are requested from suppliers who do not necessarily quote costs/prices. This can be shortlisted by removing those who do not achieve mandatory requirements. Then we can have meetings with supplier representatives in order to clarify and validate the technical proposals. The suppliers may be asked to demonstrate certain part of proposals. A <u>***Memorandum of Agreement***</u> or (MoA) may be an outcome of these meetings. This is acceptance of the customer of proposed solution of offered by the supplier.
>
>In the next stage, tenders are then invited from suppliers who have signed MoAs. The tenders would incorporate the MoA and will be concerned with the financial terms of the potential contract.

## Evaluation of Proposals
The process of evaluation may include: 
* scrutiny of proposal documents
* interviewing suppliers' representatives
* demonstrations
* site visits
* practical tests

# Terms of a Contract
## Definitions
The terminology used in the contract document may need to be defined. who is meant by the words 'client' and 'supplier'
## Form of Agreement
Is it a contract of sale, a lease or a licence? Can the subject of the contract e.g. licence be transferred to another party?
## Goods and Services to be supplied
Equipment and software to be supplied. This would be a list of the individual pieces of equipment, complete with specific model numbers. 
*Services to be provided: (this would include things like)*
* training
* documentation
* installation
* conversion of existing files
* maintenance agreements
* transitional insurance agreements 
## Ownership of Software
Who has ownership of the software? Can the customer sell the software to others? 
Can the supplier sell the software to others?. For OTS systems this is normally just a licence to use the software. Whereas for bespoke systems, the customer may have exclusive use of the software. They can ensure this by acquiring copyright to the software or by specifying in contract that they should have exclusive use. 
## Environment
Where physical equipment is to be installed, the line between customer/supplier responsibilities with regard to accommodation and electrical supply are specified.
## Customer Commitments
The customer may have to provide accommodation for the suppliers and perhaps facilities like telephone lines.
## Acceptance Procedures
This would specify details user acceptance tests. Time required to conduct these tests. Deliverables on which these will be performed. Procedure for signing off the testing as completed.
## Standards
A customer may require the supplier to comply with a certain standard. e.g. ISO 12207 etc. 
## Project and Quality Management
Arrangements for the management of the project. Frequency and nature of progress meetings. Progress information to be supplied to customer
## Timetable
This provides a schedule of when the key parts of the projects should be completed. 
## Price and Payment Method
When the payments are to be made. How will they be made.

# Contract Management
This is the process of overseeing the relationship and work between a customer and the supplier from the moment of signing the contract until the completion of the project.
