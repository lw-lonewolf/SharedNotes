# Why Do We Need Software Project Management
Put simply, without proper software project management, most SP projects exceed their time or budget constraints and have a risk of outright failure. The reason of project shortcomings is normally *lack of skill and proven approach towards software project management.*
# What is defined as a Project?
Most dictionaries mention project as a "planned" activity, or a 'specific plan or design'. The important part of why we need a project is to plan for what we're about to do. i.e. think carefully about something before doing it.
## Characteristics of a Project
* non-routine tasks
* planning is required
* has a predetermined time constraint
* has limited/restricted access to resources
* work is carried out for someone other than yourself (a customer exists)
* the project is large/complex
* work involves different specialisms/specialists 
# How are Software Projects Different?
There are four major characteristics that set software projects different from traditional projects: 
1) **Invisibility:** In a traditional project, you can visually/physically see progress e.g. while constructing a bridge, you can see how much progress has been made. On the other hand Software Projects are invisible i.e. their progress 
2) **Conformity:** A software engineer doesn't have consistent rules to conform to like traditional engineers. They must conform to the requirements of human clients. It is not just that individuals can be inconsistent, organizations as well sometimes due to internal communications and so on exhibit "organizational stupidity".
3) **Flexibility:** The software is easy to change normally and is considered an advantage, when it interfaces with a physical or organizational system, its expected it shall adapt to this new connection and not vice versa.
4) **Complexity:** Per dollar, pound or euro spent, Software Products contain more complexity than other engineered artifacts.

# Activities of SPM
There are three successive processes in bringing a new system to life.
1) **The Feasibility Study**
2) **Planning**
3) **Execution**
### Feasibility Study
This assesses whether the project is worth starting - that it has valid **business case**. Information is collected about the requirements of the proposed application. Requirements elicitation can be initially complex and difficult. The stakeholders may know the aims they wish to pursue but have no idea about means of achievments. The developmental and operational costs and value of the benefits of the new system, will also have to be estimated.
### Planning
If the feasibility study indicates that the projects appears to viable then project planning can begin. For larger projects we would not do all of the planning in the very beginning and instead will create **an outline plan for the whole project** and **detailed one for the first stage.** The detailed planning of other stages are then put off to a time nearer to their initiation.
### Project Execution
The project can now be executed. The execution of a project often contains *design* and *implementation* sub-phases. The *design* part refers to decisions about the form of the *products* to be created. This could relate to the external appearance of the software or the internal architecture. The plan details the "activities" to be conducted.
