 Let $m$, $n$, $r$ be non-negative integers with r not exceeding either m or n. Then 

$$\binom{m+n} {r}=\sum_{k \,=\, 0}^{r} \binom{m} {r-k} \binom{n} {k}$$

#### Theorem 4: 

Let $n$ and $r$ be non-negative integers with $r \leq n$ . Then
$$
{\binom{n+1} {r+1}}=\sum_{j=r}^{n} {\binom{j} {r}}. 
$$
counts the bit strings of length $n+1$ Proof: We use a combinatorial proof.The left-hand side, $\binom{n+1} {r+1}$ containing $r+1$ ones. We show that the right-hand side counts the same objects by considering the cases corresponding to the possible locations of the final 1 in a string with $r+1$ ones. This final one must occur at position $r+1, r+2, \ldots,$ or $n+1$ Furthermore, if the last one is the $k^{th}$ bit there must be $r$ ones among the first $k-1$ positions. Consequently, by Example 14 in Section 6.3, there are $\binom{k-1} {r}$ such bit strings. Summing over $k$ with $r+1 \leq k \leq n+1$ , we find that there are
$$
\sum_{k=r+1}^{n+1} \binom{k-1} {r}=\sum_{j=r}^{n} \binom{j} {r} 
$$
