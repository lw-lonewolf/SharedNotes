$a \equiv b$ (mod $m$)

If a and b are integers and m is a positive integer, then a is "congruent" to b modulo m if 

$m$ _divides_ $(a-b)$

$a \equiv b$ (mod $m$) iff  $a$ mod $m$ = $b$ mod $m$
>$a+c \equiv b+d (\text{mod}m)$

if $a \equiv b$(mod $m$) and $c \equiv d$ (mod $m$), then 
> $ac \equiv bd$ (mod $m$)
