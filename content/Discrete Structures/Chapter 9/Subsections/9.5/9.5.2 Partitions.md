A partition of a set $S$ is a collection of disjoint nonempty subsets of $S$ that have $S$ as their union. In other words, the collection of subsets $A_{i}$, $i \in I$ (where $I$ is an index set) forms a partition of $S$ if and only if $Ai \ne \phi \quad \text{for} \quad i ∈ I$, $Ai ∩ Aj = ∅$ $when$ $i \ne j$, 
![[Partition.png]]

and $\sum_{i \in I}{A_{i}} = S$
(Sum of All Partitions make up the complete set.)

Let $R$ be an equivalence relation on a set $S$. Then the equivalence classes of $R$ form a partition of S. Conversely, given a partition ${A_{i} | i \in I }$ of the set $S$, there is an equivalence relation R that has the sets $A_{i}$, $i \in I$, as its equivalence classes.
